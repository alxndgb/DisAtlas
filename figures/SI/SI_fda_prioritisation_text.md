### FDA-approved pairs scored with models trained before the first trial {#sec:SI_fda_prioritisation}

**Design.** For each FDA-approved repurposing pair in the development split, the earlier-approved indication is the anchor and the later indication is the target. Each pair is scored with the dated DisAtlas model trained only on evidence available before the target's first registered trial (primary cut-off), and, as a stricter check, before the earliest trial of either indication. The anchor is compared against every disease in that dated graph, excluding the anchor itself and any disease already trialled with a drug of the same mechanism class by that date. The endpoint is the share of targets ranked in the top 10% of these candidates (random expectation 10%). The same candidates are ranked by four comparators: disease degree, the number of graph neighbours shared with the anchor, the number of literature genes shared with the anchor (counted only up to the cut-off year), and 20 stratum-matched decoy targets per pair. Confidence intervals are cluster bootstrap intervals over drug groups (2,000 resamples). The design, endpoints and comparators were fixed before scoring, and the held-out split was not read.

**Pairs.** Of 540 development pairs, 7 have no trial date, 41 have a first trial before the earliest dated graph, 4 have an anchor and a target that map to the same DisAtlas node, 238 have an anchor or target that is not yet a node in the dated graph, and 84 have a target already trialed with a drug of the same class. That leaves 166 scorable pairs from 73 drugs at the primary cut-off and 150 pairs from 66 drugs at the stricter cut-off.

**Result.** The Supplementary Table gives every endpoint and paired difference. At the primary cut-off, DisAtlas places about two thirds of targets in the top 10% of candidates, far above matched decoy diseases and the 10% random expectation. The shared-neighbor count ranks above DisAtlas at this cut-off. Its top-10% share is higher (74.1% for the shared-neighbor count against 63.9% for DisAtlas; difference −10.2 points, 95% CI −16.6 to −1.4) and its mean percentile, which the table does not list, is also higher (DisAtlas − shared neighbors −7.2 percentile points, 95% CI −12.3 to −3.2). Shared literature genes and disease degree do not differ from DisAtlas at the primary cut-off (both intervals include zero). At the stricter cut-off, the top-10% shares of DisAtlas and the shared-neighbor count do not differ (66.7% for the shared-neighbor count against 63.3% for DisAtlas; difference −3.3 points, 95% CI −10.4 to +5.2), and DisAtlas ranks above disease degree. The shared-neighbor count keeps the higher mean percentile, again not listed in the table (−7.0 percentile points, −12.3 to −3.0). In this dated design, most of the DisAtlas signal on approved pairs is also captured by direct graph proximity to the anchor, and DisAtlas does not rank these targets above that baseline.

**Limitations sentence (main text).** On dated FDA-approved repurposing pairs, a direct count of shared graph neighbors ranked the later indication as high as or higher than DisAtlas, so the added value of DisAtlas over graph proximity is not established in that design.
